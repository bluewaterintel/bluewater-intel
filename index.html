📖 Tutorial
How to use Bluewater Intel
Welcome aboard
Bluewater Intel combines ocean data with regional fishing knowledge to show you where target species are most likely to be biting. This guide walks through the app and explains what information drives the predictions.
Getting Started
Five steps to your first prediction.
1
Pick your home port
Tap HOME PORT in the top bar and pick the port you're fishing from. The map zooms to your area and a range circle shows how far offshore is reachable. 95 ports are supported from Portsmouth, NH to San Diego, CA (plus Bahamas crossings). Until you pick a port, no port shows as "home" on the chart — your home port appears as a larger gold anchor badge with a white ring, and fishing spots only load once a port is selected.
2
Pick your target species
Tap TARGET SPECIES and choose what you're after. 50 species are in the picker — each has its own habitat preferences (depth, temperature range, structure type, thermal-break tolerance). If you pick a species that doesn't live near your port, the app will tell you so directly.
3
Read the Bite Map
Colored areas show where your target species is most likely to be biting right now. The scale goes from green (decent) → yellow → orange → red (best). Tap any colored area for a breakdown of why that spot scored well — and tap the 🔊 button in that breakdown to have it read aloud.
Planning ahead? Use the Forecast pills on the bite-score banner (Now / +12h / +24h) to see how wind, tide, solunar, and pressure shift the score — without leaving the map.
Planning a specific day? Tap any waypoint and open the 6-Day Forecast tab for a 3-hour breakdown of wind, seas, air, water, and the tides — so you can pick which day and tide to run.
Two numbers, two meanings:
Bite Score = how favorable conditions are at that spot (the big colored number).
Confidence = how sure the model is about that score — in either direction (the smaller number next to it).
Confidence rises when the major signals agree and falls when they conflict. It also climbs when we're confident a spot is poor — for example, a species that's out of season or out of its range here is a high-confidence "stay home," not a maybe. The "What's affecting confidence" panel in any breakdown explains what's driving the number.
4
Use the right-side icon column
🗺 Base Map — switch between Satellite, Ocean Bathymetric (NOAA BlueTopo), or Seafloor Relief. Toggle Depth Contours in Layers as a transparent overlay on any basemap.
◆ Layers — iOS-style toggles grouped into Predictions, Map Markers, Ocean Data, and an Advanced section. Toggle the Bite Map (the color-coded bite forecast for your target species), Major Fishing Areas (canyons, wrecks, reefs, lumps and ledges — the 15 closest to your port), ports, your catches, satellite SST & Chlorophyll, animated Wind, Front Convergence (SSH), and ocean currents. Closures and LORAN-C live under Advanced.
📏 Ruler — tap to measure distance in nautical miles. Drop multiple points to plot a route.
⚙ Settings — set defaults like home port, species, base map, and account preferences.
5
Explore the menu
Tap MENU in the top right. It's organized into grouped sections like iOS Settings: Map & Data (Offshore Chart, Fishing Reports), My Tools (My Catches with logging + pattern analytics, Waypoints & Structure, AI Catch Measure, Virtual Tackle Box, Download My Trip), and Reference (Regulations, Terminal Tackle, Fish Encyclopedia, Tutorial, Help & Contact). Ocean layers — SST, chlorophyll, wind, radar, currents, and front convergence — are toggled on the map itself under Layers. To log a catch, open My Catches and tap Log a Catch.

🎣
Catch logging with auto-filled conditions
Open Menu → My Catches and tap Log a Catch to record one. Choose species, size, time, and location (map center or GPS). The app reads SST, moon phase, tide stage, pressure trend, and wind direction from the same data sources driving the Bite Map — so your catch record is internally consistent with what the algorithm showed. Optionally attach a photo and opt in to share the catch as an anonymous fishing report (which then feeds back into predictions). Once you've logged a handful, a Your Patterns panel mines your catches for the conditions that produce fish — best moon phase, tide, pressure trend, wind, water-temp band, and time of day — and can break it down per species.
⚖️
Marine closures & regulations
Toggle Closures & Sanctuaries in the Layers panel to see permanent spatial closures on the chart — National Marine Sanctuaries (orange), no-take reserves (red solid), HAPCs (purple dashed), and groundfish closures (red dashed). Tap any closure for the rules and a link to the official regulation page. Menu → Regulations opens a full directory of federal sources (NMFS, MAFMC, SAFMC, Gulf SERO, Pacific WCR) and every state agency from Maine to Southern California, with your home state's agency surfaced at the top.
🛰
Satellite ocean data overlays
Two NASA satellite layers, toggleable in the Layers panel under "OCEAN DATA": Sea Surface Temp (MUR 1km — cloud-free, hi-res, shows the Gulf Stream wall and temp breaks) and Chlorophyll-A (VIIRS 1km — water color/productivity, where bait stacks along the green-to-blue boundary). These are the same data products the bite score is computed from, so the map matches the forecast. Both work on any basemap. The color legend appears at top-left when active. ~1 day processing lag is normal for satellite ocean data.
💨
Animated wind layer
Toggle Wind (animated) under Ocean Data for a live particle wind field — streaks show direction and are colored by speed (calm green → strong red). Data comes from the GFS/HRRR seamless blend: HRRR at 3 km for nearshore waters within 48 h, then GFS offshore and beyond. For a point forecast with gusts and 6-day outlook, tap any waypoint and use the 6-Day Forecast tab.
📐
Real-feature bathymetry under the hood
Depth-driven habitat scoring is anchored to NOAA CUDEM / BlueTopo (~3 m) on US waters (ETOPO elsewhere), blended with 201 real-world reference points — canyon heads, named banks, shelf-edge stations, and Gulf canyons. The algorithm merges these with the regional shelf model so depth is correct around real features instead of just averaged. To see the seafloor, switch the basemap to Ocean Bathymetric and tap the map for depth in feet and fathoms.
What Data Drives the Predictions
The Bite Map combines all of these — none of them by itself.
🌡
Sea Surface Temperature (SST)
Every species has a temperature range where it actively feeds. Yellowfin like 70-78°F water; bluefin prefer cooler 58-68°F; sailfish want 74-82°F. SST is the single most heavily-weighted input for offshore species. Gaussian falloff: in-range = full credit, edges get partial credit, way out = zero.
📊
Temperature break
The boundary between cold and warm water — what fishermen call a "color line" or "the wall" — concentrates baitfish, which attracts predators. We measure the temperature gradient and treat sharp breaks as a strong signal for pelagics. In the bite breakdown this shows as Temperature break.
◎
Front convergence
Where a temperature break, color edge, ocean-current shear, and satellite sea-surface height (SSH) features stack together — the same convergence ROFFS and Hiltons chart for tuna. On the bite map this shows up as Front convergence in the breakdown; toggle Front Convergence (SSH) under Ocean Data to see the underlying eddies and breaks. Magenta lines mark the strongest SSH edges near your port.
⬇
Water depth (bathymetry)
Depth determines what's possible — yellowfin won't be in 30 ft of water no matter how warm it is. Each species has preferred depth bands. On US coasts we score against NOAA CUDEM (BlueTopo, ~3 m); elsewhere ETOPO fills in. Flexible species like bluefin have multiple bands (shallow shelf for NC fall blitz, deeper canyon water for summer giants, Gulf canyons for Apr-Jun spawners). Offshore pelagics are also held to blue water: the map keeps tuna and billfish out on the shelf edge and beyond instead of lighting up the inner shelf just because the surface is warm. And deep bottom species like golden tilefish are scored on an estimated bottom temperature for their depth — not the warm surface — so they correctly show up over deep structure (e.g. Norfolk Canyon) year-round.
🌿
Chlorophyll concentration
Indicates how green the water is, which drives the food chain. Inshore species like clean coastal water with some chlorophyll; offshore pelagics want clear blue water (low chlorophyll). Color edges between green and blue water often hold the most fish.
📈
Barometric pressure trend
Falling pressure (storm approaching) often triggers active feeding. Stable high pressure can shut down the bite. We factor in the recent 24-hour pressure trend.
🌙
Solunar windows, tide stage & moon phase
Major and minor solunar feeding windows are calculated from the moon's position. For inshore species especially, tide stage matters a lot — most inshore action happens on a moving tide. Moon phase itself is also weighted: new and full moons drive stronger tides and more aggressive nighttime feeding for many inshore species.
💨
Wind direction & weather change
Wind affects boat positioning, surface chop, and how bait stacks against structure. Different species have different wind preferences. Recent weather changes (a front passing through) can also trigger or kill the bite.
📅
Seasonal patterns with regional shifts
Every species has known seasonal patterns by region. Striped bass run south to NC in winter and back to MA in summer. Tuna show up in mid-Atlantic canyons starting in late spring. Tarpon migration runs Florida → Carolinas → Chesapeake on a known schedule. Latitude-shifted seasons account for the same species running 4-6 weeks later as you move north. Crucially, season and range now act as a gate: if a species is out of season or simply outside its real range for your area (for example, blackfin tuna far north of their Gulf-Stream grounds), the map suppresses it and the breakdown flags it with high confidence — rather than lighting up spots where the fish wouldn't actually be.
📍
Recent catch reports
Recent fishing reports for a species near a location bump up scores in that area. Reports decay with an ~8-hour half-life — a 2-hour-old report carries full weight, yesterday's much less. Reports that include a port (matched within ~24–90 nm of that port) move the Bite Map; posts without a usable port show in the forum but do not shift scores. Recency uses the date fished when provided.
Honest Notes & Limitations
⚠ Predictions are guidance, not guarantees
Fish move on their own schedule and even perfect-looking conditions don't always produce. The app is best used as a planning tool — pick a few high-scoring areas to start your day, then adapt based on what you see on the water.
⚠ Always verify regulations
Marine closures and regulations change throughout the year — bag limits, slot limits, seasonal openings, emergency closures. The Regulations page links to official sources. Always confirm current rules with the responsible agency before fishing.
⚠ Not a navigation tool
Bluewater Intel is a fishing planning tool — not navigation, not safety equipment. Use proper charts, GPS, marine radio, and weather radio for navigation and safety decisions. Found a bug or have feedback? Tap Menu → Help & Contact, or email info@bluewaterintel.com.
